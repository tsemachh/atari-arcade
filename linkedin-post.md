I decided to stop recreating games.

You can get *close* — but close is never as good as the real thing. The timing, the physics, that one frame of feel you remember in your fingers… a remake always loses something.

So I flipped the goal. My purpose here isn't to rebuild the games we loved — it's to make them **accessible again**, for the people who actually played them back then but aren't techy enough to wrangle an emulator, hunt down ROMs, and configure a joystick.

So I stood up and took the real thing: Avery Lee's cycle-accurate **Altirra** emulator, in its portable **AltirraSDL** form. Huge thanks to @jdebski for the AltirraSDL project — and for the genuinely prompt help getting my changes merged upstream.

To make things short: **WASM came to the rescue.** The actual emulator, compiled to WebAssembly, runs full-speed right in the browser — no installs, no downloads, no setup. Tap a game and you're playing.

My recommendation: **play it on a computer** — full screen, real keyboard, the genuine article. That's where the accuracy shows. The phone works great too (install it as an app, plays offline): each title is matched to the right joystick feel — 4-way for the maze games, 8-way for the platformers — plus tilt steering for the racers and an old-school AUTO FIRE switch under the fire button. But for the true 1983 experience, sit down at a desk, like we did.
